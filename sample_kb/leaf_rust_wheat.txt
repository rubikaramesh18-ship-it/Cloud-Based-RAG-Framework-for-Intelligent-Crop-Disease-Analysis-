Leaf Rust in Wheat

Leaf rust, caused by the fungus Puccinia triticina, is a common and widespread disease of
wheat worldwide. The most recognizable symptom is small, round to oval, orange-brown
pustules scattered on the upper surface of leaves, which release a rust-colored powdery
spore mass when touched or rubbed. As the disease progresses, pustules can merge, causing
leaves to yellow, dry out prematurely, and reduce the plant's photosynthetic capacity,
which in turn lowers grain fill and yield. Severely infected fields may show an overall
orange-brown discoloration when viewed from a distance, which is often the first clue
visible in aerial or drone imagery.

Conditions favoring outbreak: moderate temperatures (15-22 degrees Celsius), free moisture
on leaves from dew or light rain, and dense canopy with prolonged leaf wetness duration.

Recommended management:
1. Plant resistant or tolerant wheat varieties suited to the local region.
2. Apply triazole or strobilurin fungicides at early disease onset, especially before
   flag-leaf emergence when yield impact is highest.
3. Monitor fields regularly during the growing season, particularly after rain or heavy dew.
4. Avoid late, dense sowing that increases humidity within the canopy.
5. Destroy volunteer wheat plants and crop residue that can harbor rust spores between
   seasons.
6. Rotate fungicide classes to reduce the risk of resistance developing in rust populations.
