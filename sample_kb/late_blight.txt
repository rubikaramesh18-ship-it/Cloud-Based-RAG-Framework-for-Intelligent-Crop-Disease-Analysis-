Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) in Potato and Tomato

Late blight is a fungal-like (oomycete) disease that spreads rapidly in cool, wet weather.
Early symptoms appear as small, water-soaked, pale green to brown spots on leaves, usually
starting at the leaf edges or tips. Under humid conditions, a white fuzzy mold can be seen
on the underside of affected leaves. As the disease progresses, lesions enlarge, turn dark
brown to black, and entire leaves can collapse and rot within days. On tomato and potato
stems, dark brown lesions may girdle the stem. On tubers and fruit, the disease causes a
reddish-brown, granular dry rot beneath the skin.

Conditions favoring outbreak: prolonged leaf wetness, temperatures between 10-24 degrees
Celsius, high humidity above 90 percent, and dense canopy with poor airflow.

Recommended management:
1. Remove and destroy infected plant material immediately; do not compost it.
2. Apply a protectant fungicide (e.g., copper-based or chlorothalonil) preventively before
   symptoms appear if weather conditions favor the disease.
3. Improve field drainage and plant spacing to reduce humidity around the canopy.
4. Avoid overhead irrigation; irrigate at the base of the plant in the morning so foliage
   dries quickly.
5. Rotate crops and avoid planting potato/tomato in the same field in consecutive seasons.
6. Use certified disease-free seed potatoes and resistant tomato varieties where available.
