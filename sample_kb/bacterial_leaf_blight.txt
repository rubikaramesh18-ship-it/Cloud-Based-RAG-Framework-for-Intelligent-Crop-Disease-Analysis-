Bacterial Leaf Blight in Rice

Bacterial leaf blight, caused by Xanthomonas oryzae, is one of the most destructive bacterial
diseases of rice, particularly in irrigated and rainfed lowland areas. Symptoms typically
begin as water-soaked, pale yellow to white streaks near the leaf tip or margins, which
enlarge and turn yellow to greyish-white as the disease develops. In severe cases, entire
leaves wither and turn a pale straw color, a stage known as "kresek" when it occurs in
seedlings, often leading to plant death. A milky, sticky bacterial exudate (ooze) may be
visible on the underside of severely infected leaves in early morning.

Conditions favoring outbreak: warm temperatures (25-34 degrees Celsius), high humidity,
strong wind and rain (which spreads bacteria and creates wounds for entry), and excessive
nitrogen fertilization.

Recommended management:
1. Use certified, disease-free seed and resistant rice varieties where available.
2. Avoid excessive nitrogen application; apply fertilizer in split doses.
3. Maintain proper field drainage and avoid deep standing water for long periods.
4. Avoid working in fields when leaves are wet to reduce mechanical spread of bacteria.
5. Remove and destroy infected stubble and volunteer rice plants after harvest.
6. Apply copper-based bactericides only as advised, since chemical control alone is often
   insufficient without resistant varieties and good field sanitation.
