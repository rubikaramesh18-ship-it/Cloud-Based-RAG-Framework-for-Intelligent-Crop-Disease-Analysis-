Powdery Mildew in Cucurbits, Wheat, and Grapes

Powdery mildew is caused by several related fungal species and is one of the most widespread
and easily recognized plant diseases. It appears as white to greyish, talcum-powder-like
spots or patches on the upper and lower surfaces of leaves, stems, and sometimes fruit.
As the infection spreads, the powdery coating can cover the entire leaf surface, causing
leaves to yellow, curl, become distorted, and eventually die. Unlike many fungal diseases,
powdery mildew does not require free water on the leaf surface to infect -- it thrives in
warm, dry daytime conditions combined with high humidity and shade at night.

Conditions favoring outbreak: moderate temperatures (15-27 degrees Celsius), high relative
humidity but dry leaf surfaces, poor air circulation, and dense or shaded canopy.

Recommended management:
1. Choose resistant or tolerant crop varieties when planting.
2. Prune and space plants to improve airflow and reduce humidity within the canopy.
3. Apply sulfur-based or potassium bicarbonate fungicides at first sign of infection.
4. Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization, which promotes soft, susceptible new growth.
5. Remove and destroy heavily infected leaves to reduce spore load.
6. Monitor fields regularly during warm, humid periods since the disease spreads quickly
   once established.
